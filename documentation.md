## Getting the tracker running

How the Orlosky 3D tracker works:

1. Convert the frame to grayscale, find the darkest area (assumed to be the pupil).
2. Threshold at three levels (darkest +5, +15, +25) and keep the biggest dark blob at each.
3. Fit an ellipse to each blob and keep the one with the best "goodness" score.
4. The % on screen is the share of the outline pixels lying close to the fitted ellipse. At 85% or more,
   the ellipse is stored as a "ray"; where rays cross is the estimated eye centre. From the eye centre and
   the pupil the 3D gaze vector is computed and written to `gaze_vector.txt` every frame.

### numpy 2.x broke pupil detection (earlier)
- **Symptom:** a huge blue circle and a chaotic yellow ellipse.
- **Cause:** `get_darkest_area` adds `uint8` pixel values, which overflow differently in numpy 2.
- **Fix (Worked):** pin `numpy==1.26.4` and `opencv-python==4.11.0.86` in `requirements.txt`.

### "Gaze: no signal" in the app although the tracker ran (earlier)
- **Cause:** the tracker was started from the repo root, so `gaze_vector.txt` was written there while the
  app was watching a different folder.
- **Fix (Worked):** run the app with `--gaze-file gaze_vector.txt` (documented in `app/README.md`).

### Frozen or stale gaze signal (earlier)
- **Cause:** the bridge between the file and the app only published a new position when the value changed,
  so a steady eye looked like a dead signal.
- **Fix (Worked):** publish on a timer as well as on change.

### "area was 0" printed in the tracker window (earlier)
- Means the ellipse check found nothing for that frame (pupil hidden, blink). Normal when occasional; only
  a problem if constant.

## Web App Decisions

### 1. First version
- Flask server with a live stream to the browser (`/api/stream`), a vocabulary file (`vocab.json`) with
  categories and words, and gaze-driven selection by dwelling on a box.
- Words are spoken from pre-generated audio (`tools/make_audio.py`), with the browser's speech synthesis as
  the fallback.
- Input can be gaze (from the tracker) or the mouse (for testing without the glasses).
- `tools/simulate_gaze.py` fakes a gaze signal for testing.

### 2. Calibration, version 1: timed (first iteration)
- Nine dots, each shown for a fixed time (settle 0.8 s, collect 1 s). A least-squares fit maps the
  tracker's raw angles (yaw, pitch) to screen position; an affine or a quadratic fit is chosen by
  leave-one-out error. Error is reported as a percentage of screen width.
- **Verdicts:** up to 5% good, up to 10% usable, above 10% poor.
- **Result (Did not work well):** too quick for the eye to settle; it often reported "poor".

### 3. Calibration, version 2: step by step, with a live gaze panel (second iteration)
- Each dot waits for the person, shows how steady the capture was, and offers Accept or Redo. A live panel
  shows the gaze vector, angles, steadiness, and a plot of the raw angles.
- **Result (Partly):** easier to follow. The remaining "poor" results looked like tracker-input problems
  (see section 7), not calibration problems.

### 4. Dwell (selection) rules
- **Problem:** a fixed 1 s dwell was too fast and reset whenever the eye drifted.
- **Rules now:** every box has its own timer. Looking away pauses it and the progress is remembered;
  progress is forgotten after 15 s; selecting anything resets all progress. After a selection a 700 ms lock
  stops accidental re-firing.
- **Times (set in `vocab.json`):** word 5 s, category 3 s, More 1.5 s, Speak/Clear/Help 3 s, video buttons
  1.5 s, volume buttons 1.5 s (hold-to-repeat every 0.6 s).
- **Paging:** up/down arrows are tiring and impossible for someone who can only move their eyes sideways.
  All categories stay on screen; four words per page with a **More** box. A horizontal-row layout is
  planned.
- **Layout:** categories left, video panel centre, words right, a bar on top (sentence, Speak, Clear,
  Help).

### 5. Help button
- A Help box that shows a banner and speaks a help phrase; can be cancelled. Audio for it is generated by
  `make_audio.py` (`ui_help.mp3`).

### 6. Video panel
- **Problem:** the app only ever showed the default video.
- **Solution:** play a **playlist** chosen by a caregiver.
- **Config file rejected:** editing `videos.json` is not friendly (not everyone can edit JSON). Replaced by a
  dialog opened with the **V** key (or the "Videos (V)" footer button) where a caregiver pastes a YouTube
  link; it is saved in `settings.json`.
- **Bug (Worked, fixed):** after saving a playlist the video sat on "Loading video..." and the page reset.
  Causes: the page reloaded on save, and the player config passed `videoId: undefined` for playlists. Fix:
  no reload, no `videoId` key for playlists, a watchdog message, and the YouTube error numbers shown.
- **Needs internet:** the YouTube embed does; the rest of the app works offline.
- The video is a fixed 16:9 box so it no longer shows black bars.

### 7. Volume controls
- Quieter / Mute / Louder with hold-to-repeat and a volume display, next to the three playback buttons.
- **Bug (Worked, fixed): 0% was still audible.** Calling `unMute()` after `setVolume(0)` restored the old
  volume. Now 0% mutes the player.
- **Bug (Open): playback speeds up when clicking Quieter in mouse mode.** Could not be reproduced. A guard
  that resets the speed was added; the details are still needed if it returns.

### 8. Calibration, version 3: jump, back, restart, colour border (2026-11-23)
- **Problems:** the 9-dot run could not be restarted halfway, a single dot could only be redone after all 9
  were captured, and the green/orange/red status border was only on the gaze-vector panel.
- **Change:** all 9 circles are always shown and can be clicked (or keys 1-9); **B** goes back, **C**
  restarts; the next dot after an accept is the next one still missing; the status colour now frames the
  whole page.
- **Status (Worked in tests with a simulated eye; not yet judged on the real tracker).**

## Hardware finding: IR LED reflections

(2026-11-23) The camera has two IR LEDs next to the lens. With the camera right in front of the eye, the
eye reflects them back (the "glint"), which disturbs the tracker.

Physics, for later reference: LEDs on the camera axis cause the bright-pupil effect and glints; the glint
moves onto the pupil as the eye turns toward the LED; offsetting the LED vertically makes the glint miss
the pupil.

- **Tried:** black tape over one LED, camera on the left side of the left eye.
- **Result (Partly):** the pupil is dark with one glint. Looking away from the light, confidence reaches
  100%. At the centre or toward the light it drops a lot and the ellipse shakes even when the eye is still.

## Fixing the pupil outline (2026-11-23 to 2026-11-24)

### Attempt A: glint removal and convex-hull fill (Removed)
- Idea: paint over small bright spots near the pupil before thresholding, and fill notches in the outline
  with its convex hull.
- On a synthetic eye, edge-glint confidence rose from 87-90% to 96%. On the repo test video it was about
  the same. A looser hull limit (0.80) raised the % but made the shake worse (1.11 px to 1.49 px); the
  default was tightened to 0.97.
- On the real camera the glint was painted over (confirmed in a debug window) but the ellipse still
  wandered, so the glint was not the main cause. Removed.

### Attempt B: smoothing the pupil centre (Removed)
- A median of the last 5 centres plus blending. It only changes the gaze centre, not the yellow ellipse
  that was wandering, so there was no visible effect. Removed.

### Attempt C: hold the last good pupil while confidence is under 60% (Removed)
- On the test video shake dropped (1.10 px to 0.74 px), but on the real camera the ellipse looked choppy
  because it froze for up to 15 frames. A frozen ellipse is worse in use than a noisy one. Removed.
- Bugs on the way: a missing `global` line gave `UnboundLocalError` in `process_frames`. Rule: any
  variable assigned inside a function needs a `global` line there, including in `reset_tracking_state`.

### Debug view and the real cause
- Showing the three threshold images in separate windows revealed the actual problem: a **tall, soft bright
  reflection covers the right third of the pupil** at some angles. Only the dark left crescent passed the
  threshold, so the fitted ellipse was too narrow and sat left of the real pupil (confidence about 46%).
  Raising the thresholds did not help; the outline leaked into the iris.

### Attempt D: edge fallback (Worked, usable, not perfect, kept)
- When the normal confidence is under 75%, fit the ellipse only to outline points that lie on a sharp
  dark-to-light edge and are not next to a bright spot, ignoring the outline that runs through the
  reflection's haze.
- On a frame rebuilt from a screenshot the ellipse moved from the left crescent (x about 278, 51 x 95 px)
  to the real pupil (x about 300, 103 x 119 px). Test video: mean confidence 80% to 86%, shake 1.11 px to
  1.19 px (slightly worse), about +1 ms per frame. On the real camera: "works better, not perfect, but
  usable".
- Settings at the top of the file: `EDGE_FALLBACK_ENABLED`, `EDGE_FALLBACK_BELOW`, `EDGE_MIN_GRADIENT`,
  `EDGE_BRIGHT`, `EDGE_MIN_POINTS`.
- **Limits:** if the reflection covers most of the visible pupil edge there are too few edge points and the
  old (wrong) ellipse is kept. During fallback frames the on-screen % is the share of the outline matching
  the edge-fitted ellipse, not the original measure.
